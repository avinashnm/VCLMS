2 BACKGROUND STUDY
2.1 Brain Tumor Diagnosis and Hospital Management Systems
      Brain tumor diagnosis is a complex and time-sensitive medical process that plays a crucial role in determining patient treatment and survival outcomes. Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) is the primary imaging technique used for brain tumor diagnosis due to its ability to provide detailed visualization of brain tissues. In most hospitals, MRI scans are analyzed manually by radiologists, who identify tumor regions based on experience and visual interpretation.Along with diagnosis, hospitals also manage patient records, medical reports, appointments, and follow-ups using separate systems or partially manual processes. Diagnosis, report handling, and appointment scheduling are often disconnected, leading to inefficiencies in healthcare delivery. The absence of a unified system makes it difficult for patients to track their medical progress and for doctors to manage diagnostic results and appointments effectively.

2.2 Automated Tumor Detection and Deep Learning-Based Analysis
  Recent advancements in artificial intelligence have introduced automated approaches for medical image analysis, particularly in brain tumor detection. Deep learning models such as Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) are capable of learning complex features from MRI scans and identifying tumor patterns without manual feature extraction. These models reduce dependency on human interpretation and improve consistency in diagnosis.
		More advanced architectures like U-Net and Attention U-Net have been specifically designed for biomedical image segmentation. U-Net performs pixel-wise segmentation, allowing precise identification of tumor regions such as edema, tumor core, and enhancing tumor. Attention U-Net further improves segmentation accuracy by focusing on relevant tumor regions and suppressing background noise, which is especially useful for detecting small but critical regions like Enhancing Tumor (ET). These automated techniques significantly enhance diagnostic accuracy and reduce analysis time compared to traditional manual methods.

		Despite advancements in deep learning-based tumor detection, many existing systems focus only on image analysis and lack integration with real-world hospital workflows. Conventional hospital systems suffer from fragmented data storage, absence of centralized dashboards, and limited automation in report generation and appointment management. Tumor detection results are often handled separately from patient portals, leading to communication gaps between patients, doctors, and administrators. There is a growing need for an integrated medical system that combines automated tumor detection with a Doctor–Patient–Admin portal. Such a system enables centralized data management, role-based access control, real-time report generation, appointment scheduling, and health recommendations within a single platform. An integrated solution improves healthcare efficiency, enhances transparency, reduces workload for medical professionals, and ensures timely diagnosis and treatment planning for patients.
      
       
2.3 Limitations of Conventional Systems and Need for Integrated Medical Portals

      The proposed system is designed to integrate an AI-based brain tumor detection model with a centralized Doctor–Patient portal. The system supports three user roles—Patient, Doctor, and Admin—each with specific access privileges. The main objective of the design is to provide automated tumor detection, efficient report generation, and streamlined healthcare workflow through a single platform.
      The portal architecture follows a modular approach. Patients can upload MRI scans, view tumor detection results, download reports, and book appointments. Doctors can access patient data, review AI-generated segmentation results, manage appointments, and provide health tips. The Admin module oversees user management, appointment monitoring, and system maintenance. Role-based access control ensures data security and privacy.
      The tumor detection system uses deep learning models such as CNN, U-Net, and Attention U-Net. MRI scans are preprocessed through normalization, resizing, and slice selection before being passed to the segmentation models. U-Net performs pixel-wise segmentation, while Attention U-Net improves accuracy by focusing on relevant tumor regions, especially the Enhancing Tumor.

2.4 Evaluation Metrics for Tumor Segmentation
  Evaluation metrics play a crucial role in assessing the performance of deep learning models used for brain tumor segmentation. These metrics help in determining how accurately the predicted tumor regions match the actual ground truth provided in the dataset. One of the most important metrics used in medical image segmentation is the Dice Score, which measures the overlap between the predicted segmentation and the ground truth. A Dice Score value closer to 1 indicates a high level of similarity and better segmentation performance, while a value closer to 0 indicates poor overlap. In addition to Dice Score, accuracy is also used to evaluate the overall correctness of the model’s predictions. However, in the case of medical image segmentation, accuracy alone may not be sufficient, as it does not consider the imbalance between tumor and non-tumor regions. Therefore, metrics such as precision and recall are also considered. Precision measures the proportion of correctly predicted tumor pixels out of all predicted tumor pixels, while recall measures the proportion of actual tumor pixels that are correctly identified by the model. These metrics provide a more detailed understanding of model performance and help in identifying strengths and weaknesses in segmentation. By using these evaluation techniques, the system ensures reliable, consistent, and clinically meaningful results, which are essential for accurate diagnosis and effective treatment planning in brain tumor detection systems.

IMAGE_PATH = r"C:\wamp64\www\BrainTumorDetectionNew\brain_tumor_data\images"

LOAD DATA

images = []
masks = []
files = sorted(os.listdir(IMAGE_PATH))
for file in files:
img_path = os.path.join(IMAGE_PATH, file)

==============================
SPLIT
==============================
X_train, X_test, Y_train, Y_test = train_test_split(X, Y, test_size=0.1)
==============================
ATTENTION BLOCK
==============================
def attention_gate(x, g, filters):
theta_x = Conv2D(filters, 1, padding='same')(x)
phi_g = Conv2D(filters, 1, padding='same')(g)
add = Add()([theta_x, phi_g])
act = Activation('relu')(add)
psi = Conv2D(1, 1, padding='same')(act)
psi = Activation('sigmoid')(psi)
return Multiply()([x, psi])
==============================
MODEL
==============================
def build_model():
inputs = Input((128,128,1))
c1 = Conv2D(32,3,activation='relu',padding='same')(inputs)
c1 = Conv2D(32,3,activation='relu',padding='same')(c1)
p1 = MaxPooling2D()(c1)
c2 = Conv2D(64,3,activation='relu',padding='same')(p1)
c2 = Conv2D(64,3,activation='relu',padding='same')(c2)
p2 = MaxPooling2D()(c2)
c3 = Conv2D(128,3,activation='relu',padding='same')(p2)
c3 = Conv2D(128,3,activation='relu',padding='same')(c3)
u1 = UpSampling2D()(c3)
att1 = attention_gate(c2, u1, 64)
u1 = Concatenate()([u1, att1])
c4 = Conv2D(64,3,activation='relu',padding='same')(u1)
u2 = UpSampling2D()(c4)
att2 = attention_gate(c1, u2, 32)
u2 = Concatenate()([u2, att2])
c5 = Conv2D(32,3,activation='relu',padding='same')(u2)
output = Conv2D(1,1,activation='sigmoid')(c5)
return Model(inputs, output)
model = build_model()
==============================
LOSS (FIXED)
==============================
def dice_loss(y_true, y_pred):
y_true = tf.cast(y_true, tf.float32)
smooth = 1e-6
intersection = tf.reduce_sum(y_true * y_pred)
return 1 - (2. * intersection + smooth) / ( tf.reduce_sum(y_true) + tf.reduce_sum(y_pred) + smooth )
model.compile(optimizer=Adam(1e-4), loss=dice_loss, metrics=['accuracy'])
model.summary()
==============================
model.fit( X_train, Y_train, validation_data=(X_test, Y_test), epochs=10, batch_size=8 )
model.save("attention_unet_model.h5")
print("? MODEL TRAINED SUCCESSFULLY")

8 CONCLUSION
8.1 Scope of the Project
            The scope of this project is to develop a web-based Doctor–Patient Portal integrated with an AI-powered Brain Tumor Detection system. The system allows patients to register, upload MRI brain scans, receive automated tumor segmentation results, download detailed reports, and book appointments with doctors. Doctors can review AI-generated tumor results, manage appointments, and provide health recommendations, while the admin manages users and system data securely.
		The tumor detection module uses deep learning models such as CNN, U-Net, and Attention U-Net to perform pixel-wise segmentation of brain tumors from MRI scans. The system classifies tumor regions into Edema, Tumor Core, and Enhancing Tumor (ET). Performance is evaluated using Dice Score and accuracy metrics to ensure reliable predictions. Currently, the system works on 2D MRI slice-based segmentation using the BraTS dataset. However, the architecture is scalable and can be extended to advanced 3D models and real-time hospital integration in the future. 
8.2 Future Enhancements
* Implement 3D U-Net for full volumetric brain tumor segmentation. 
* Integrate U-Net for automated model optimization and improved accuracy.
* Deploy the system on cloud platforms for real-time hospital access. 
* Develop a mobile application for patient and doctor accessibility. 
* Improve Enhancing Tumor detection using advanced loss functions and attention mechanisms.
